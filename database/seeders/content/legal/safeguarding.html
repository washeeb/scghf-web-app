Draft for the trustees to review. Not published. A safeguarding policy is the foundation's most important public undertaking. This draft describes what the organisation's systems already enforce; the trustees need to add the name of the designated safeguarding lead, the reporting route, and approve it as policy.

Our commitment

We work with children, with people who are ill, with widows and with people who are poor. Every one of them has the right to be safe with us. Safeguarding is not a department; it is the responsibility of every trustee, member of staff, volunteer and partner.

What we mean by safeguarding

Protecting the people we serve from abuse, neglect, exploitation and harm — whether by somebody connected with the foundation or by anybody else — and responding properly when we learn of it.

Who works with the people we serve

Nobody works unsupervised with a child or a vulnerable adult on our behalf until we have, for that person:

  • a signed declaration that they have read this policy and disclosed anything relevant;
  • a Ghana Police Service criminal record check, renewed before it expires;
  • two references, from people who do not know each other, actually taken up and spoken to;
  • a face-to-face interview, by somebody other than the person who recruited them.

Each of these is recorded, with the evidence and the name of the person who verified it, and our systems refuse to approve a volunteer until every one is recorded. A role with no such contact — a driver, somebody folding leaflets — requires the declaration only. A record of these checks is kept for six years after a person leaves us, so that a concern raised later can be looked into against what was known at the time.

Photographs and stories

We do not publish a photograph of a child or vulnerable adult without recorded consent from them or from the adult responsible for them, and consent can be withdrawn. We remove location data from every photograph before it is published. We do not publish a child's full name with their photograph, and we do not publish anything that would let a stranger find a child. Where we publish numbers about the people we help, we withhold any figure small enough to identify somebody.

Beneficiary records

Records about the people we serve are kept on private storage, are visible only to staff whose role requires it, and access to them is logged. Health information, and anything about a person's immigration, legal or family circumstances, is treated as sensitive and shared only where the person's care requires it.

Raising a concern

If you are worried about a child or vulnerable adult in connection with our work — or about the behaviour of anybody connected with us — please tell us. Concerns go directly to our designated safeguarding lead and are not seen by general staff. You can also raise a concern anonymously through our raising a concern page. If a child or adult is in immediate danger, contact the Ghana Police Service on 191 or 18555, or the Domestic Violence and Victim Support Unit.

Every concern is recorded, taken seriously and acted on. We will never penalise anybody for raising a concern in good faith, and we will report to the police and to the Department of Social Welfare where the law or the safety of a person requires it.

Review

The trustees review this policy every year, and after any serious incident.
