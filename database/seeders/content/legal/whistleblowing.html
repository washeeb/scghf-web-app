Why this page exists

People who see something wrong inside an organisation are usually the first to know and the last to be asked. If you work with us, volunteer with us, partner with us, or are served by us, and you believe something is wrong, we want to hear it — and we want you to be able to say it without fear.

What you can raise

Anything you reasonably believe is happening or has happened in connection with our work: a risk to a child or vulnerable adult; theft, fraud or the misuse of money or goods given to us; a danger to health or safety; a breach of the law; discrimination or abuse of position; or an attempt to cover any of these up.

How to raise it

Write to us through the contact page and choose the safeguarding option; concerns sent that way are seen only by the designated person and not by general staff. If your concern is about that person, or you would rather not use the website, write to the chair of trustees at the address at the foot of this page and mark the envelope "confidential".

You may raise a concern anonymously. We will act on it as far as we can, but we cannot ask you questions or tell you what happened, so if you are able to give a way of reaching you — an email address created for the purpose is fine — it helps.

What we will do

We will acknowledge a concern within five working days, look into it, and tell you the outcome as far as confidentiality allows. Where a concern involves a crime or a risk to a person we will involve the police or the Department of Social Welfare. We keep a record of every concern and of what was done about it.

Protection

Nobody who raises a concern in good faith will be treated less favourably for doing so, whether or not the concern turns out to be founded. Ghana's Whistleblower Act, 2006 (Act 720) protects people who disclose wrongdoing in good faith, and we will honour both its letter and its intent.
