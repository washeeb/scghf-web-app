# 12. Live chat

*Inbox → Live chat.* A visitor presses the **Chat** button in the corner of
any page, types a message, and it arrives here. You answer from this
screen; they see your reply on their page within a few seconds. Nothing
goes through an outside chat company — the conversation is in the
foundation's own database.

## Being "online"

The chat button on the site says **We are online** only while somebody has
the *Live chat* screen (or a chat) open in this panel. Close the tab and,
three minutes later, the site says **We are away** and shows the away
message instead. Nobody is ever told a person is waiting when nobody is.

Whether or not somebody is online, every new chat is **emailed** to the
chat address (*Site settings → Live chat → Tell this address when a chat
starts*; empty uses the general enquiries address), so a chat started at
night is in the inbox in the morning.

## Answering

1. The list shows open chats, the one that has waited longest at the top,
   with a bell on any that has an unanswered message. The number on the
   menu is how many are waiting.
2. Open one. Type in the box and press **Enter** to send. The first person
   to reply becomes the one "with" the chat; **Take this chat** does the
   same without sending.
3. **Close chat** when it is done. The visitor sees a closing line and, if
   they gave an email address and the setting is on, receives the
   transcript by email. A closed chat can be reopened.

A visitor can also end the chat themselves. Once closed, nobody can add to
it — a new question starts a new chat.

## What you can change

Everything the visitor reads is under *Site settings → Live chat*: the
button label, the panel title, the greeting, the away message and hours,
the field labels, the closing lines, which corner the button sits in,
whether an email address is required, and whether the transcript is sent.
**Show the chat** switches the button off site-wide without losing the
conversations already in the inbox.

## What is kept, and for how long

A chat is a conversation, not a case file. It is kept for **twelve months**
from its last line and then deleted, messages and all, by the retention
run. If something said in a chat needs to be kept — a complaint, a
safeguarding concern, a pledge — put it where it belongs: the contact
inbox, a beneficiary case, a pledge record.

Who can do what: **Support**, **Admin** and **Super Admin** can read and
answer. Closing or reassigning somebody else's chat needs *chat.manage*,
which Admin and Super Admin hold.
